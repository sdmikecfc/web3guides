# Model Kombat toy design standard

Approved direction from Mike, 8 September 2026.

The test is: **does this look like a toy a kid would want?** Functional geometry,
more polygons, or passing a performance check do not answer that question.

The existing garage moulds are the visual foundation: appealing silhouettes,
deliberate proportions, small readable faces, winding keys, cream mechanisms,
brass joints, and the familiar paint colours. Avoid generic upright boxes,
oversized googly eyes, floating eyebrows, and rows of cartoon teeth.

## Strength must be visible

Basic parts remain approachable. Tier 3 and 4 are where the menacing equipment
belongs. A fully upgraded machine must look fully upgraded at normal fight
distance, even without a stat label.

Mike's knight reference describes the frame and presence, not an art-style
change or an asset to copy. Use a broad chest, substantial shoulders, powerful
arms, large planted boots, and a protective helmet with a narrow, intentional
face opening. Higher-tier silhouettes must be authored, not made by scaling
the whole starter robot uniformly or sticking angry eyebrows on it.

- Tier 1: friendly, simple toy construction with visible mechanisms.
- Tier 2: reinforced parts and modest added protection; still clearly a starter's relative.
- Tier 3: broader frame, substantial shoulder armour, more protective helmet,
  braced legs and visibly capable weapons.
- Tier 4: unmistakable top-tier presence. A powerful helmet silhouette,
  layered shoulder/chest armour, larger forearms and boots, carefully placed
  brass accents. Remain a desirable sculpted toy; avoid realistic military hardware.

Every part remains independent. A tier 4 helmet on a basic body must fit and
look like one premium part, not silently upgrade the other pieces. Fast and
precision builds need their own premium silhouettes; they should not all
become the same heavy knight.

## Plain-language examples

- Heavy hitter: hammer and shield, strong armour and impact, slower recovery.
- Fast fighter: shock baton and spring legs, quick attacks and dodges, less armour.
- Sharpshooter: rifle and deliberate aim, effective at distance, needs space.

These are starting combinations, not locked classes. Parts and their actual
stats define a mixed build. Internal identifiers may remain brute/hotshot/deadeye
for compatibility; player-facing names must explain the distinction.

## Current implementation boundary

The lab now reuses the original garage moulds. The tier 3–4 silhouette direction
above is approved design guidance, **not an implemented progression, unlock,
or reward system**. Do not describe the current lab presets as maxed equipment.
Author and visually review the premium modular parts before adding that claim.
